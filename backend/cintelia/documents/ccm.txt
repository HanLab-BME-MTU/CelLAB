Convergent cross mapping (CCM) is a statistical technique used to detect causal relationships in complex dynamical systems, particularly when traditional correlation methods fail. Developed by George Sugihara and colleagues, CCM works by testing whether the historical record of one variable can be used to reliably estimate past values of another variable, with accuracy improving as more data becomes available—this "convergence" indicates causation. Unlike traditional correlation, CCM can distinguish causation from mere correlation and works even when variables don't interact contemporaneously, making it especially valuable for analyzing ecological systems, climate data, neuroscience, and other fields where feedback loops and nonlinear dynamics are common. The method is grounded in dynamical systems theory and Takens' embedding theorem, which shows that the state of a coupled system leaves imprints across all its variables.
